# Christi Reid — LinkedIn Founder Profile Copy

*Complete replacement copy for linkedin.com/in/christireid, written under the site's voice
rules and claims discipline. Grounded in current research (June 2026): LinkedIn distributes
personal-profile posts 5–10x wider than company-page posts (pages now reach ~2–5% of
followers organically), the About section truncates at ~300 characters before "see more,"
Featured content earns ~30% longer profile view time, and outcome-plus-ICP headlines
outperform job titles. Generic advice the research also surfaced — emoji for rhythm,
hashtag piles, engagement-bait questions — is deliberately rejected: it contradicts the
brand and reads as everyone else.*

**Voice rule (owner decision): "I" speaks about Christi; "we" speaks about the practice.**

> Most AI pilots die between the strategy and the build. I started Code & Clarity because the advice business was built backwards: strategy people who never deployed anything, advising engineering teams on deployment.
>
> We're the other way around — engineers who consult.
>
> I spent 10+ years shipping production software, designing developer education, and editing technical writing before consulting on any of it. That history is the method: the implementation strategies come from someone who has lived inside production systems, the training comes from someone who has taught working engineers, and the recommendations read clearly because, for years, clarity was the job.
>
> What we do, in one room:
>
> AI Implementation Strategy — which platform, which partner, what scope, at what three-year cost. Vendor, custom, or hybrid, recommended on fit and TCO at real headcount, with acceptance criteria precise enough to hold whoever builds it accountable.
>
> AI Audits & Governance — we read your codebase, not just your org chart, and the engagement ends in a decision: scoped, priced, executable. EU AI Act mapped before the regulator asks.
>
> AI Training & Capability — designed by engineers who use these tools daily. The test isn't the workshop survey; it's whether your team still uses the workflows six months later.
>
> We don't take the build. The recommendation has nothing to sell but the answer — which is also why we'll tell you when the answer is "you don't need us yet."
>
> Who hires us: mid-market SaaS companies, universities, and PE-backed portfolios with a real AI decision in front of them. Who shouldn't: if you need a Big Four logo for political cover, buy the logo; if you need a $5K chatbot configured, a freelancer is the right call — and I'll say so.
>
> Every engagement starts with a 45-minute diagnostic. You bring the problem; you leave with a straight answer — whether we can help, what it would produce, and what it costs.
>
> codeclarity.ai · hello@codeclarity.ai

## 6. Skills, recommendations, activity

- **Pin top 5 skills:** AI Strategy · AI Governance · Vendor Evaluation · Technical Training · Solution Architecture. (Skills are search metadata, not prose — these are the five your buyers type.)
- **Recommendations:** ask 2–3 former engineering/teaching colleagues to speak to *how you work* (rigor, teaching, straight answers) — colleague recommendations are credible now; client ones can come later under the same claims discipline as the site (nothing you couldn't stand behind under questioning).
- **Activity discipline:** your posts are the practice's distribution (5–10x page reach). Cadence: 1–3 posts/week using a 3-2-1 monthly mix — 3 that teach (a build-vs-buy heuristic, a TCO trap, a governance question boards should ask), 2 that annotate a cited source (MIT, McKinsey, DataCamp — receipts, never vibes), 1 that tells an honest-disqualification story ("we told them not to hire us — here's why"). Reshare every company-page post with one added sentence of your own; engage in the first hour after posting (the algorithm's evaluation window).
- **Never:** emoji, hashtag piles, "Agree?"-bait, invented percentages, AI-sounding listicles. If a post could appear on any consultancy's feed, cut it.
